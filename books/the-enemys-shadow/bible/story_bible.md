# The Enemy's Shadow — Story Bible

**Genre:** Historical thriller / erotic literary fiction
**Setting:** Occupied Paris (Jan–Apr 1943) and occupied Warsaw (May 1943 – Aug 1944)
**Structure:** 3 parts, 25 chapters, epilogue, + a note on the history · **155,102 words** · 348 pages typeset · per chapter mostly 4,600-6,300, with the climax units (ch.22-25) longer
**POV:** Third person limited, single POV (Krystyna), past tense, close psychic distance
**Central question:** *What does it cost to be loved by the only person you are using?*

---

## 1. The Lie and the Truth

| | |
|---|---|
| **Public identity** | Marion Herschlag — Alsatian-born widow of a Strasbourg industrialist; "kept" woman of Oberst Friedrich von Reuth; the prettiest ornament in German-occupied Paris, and then in Warsaw |
| **True identity** | Krystyna Stankiewiczówna, 26, born Warsaw 1917; daughter of a ruined magnate family; agent of the Polish Underground (AK), codename **WESPER** |
| **Cover route** | Recruited in 1940 while working as a translator/interpreter for a German requisition office in Kraków; two years of "training" in the beds of unimportant men; placed beside von Reuth in Paris in October 1942 |
| **Her method** | Intimacy as intelligence. The body as a filing cabinet. She never keeps a written word of her own. She never loves: *Rule Four* |

**What she trades for secrets:** access. Documents in a briefcase, a safe, a coat pocket, a mouth unguarded in the hour after. Everything she carries out of the bedroom is memorised, or photographed on a camera the size of a matchbox.

## 2. Cast

### The occupier
- **Oberst (later Generalmajor) Friedrich von Reuth**, 54. Chief of the Abwehrstelle Paris, later head of intelligence for the Warsaw garrison district ("the Blue Palace"). Widower (Anneliese, d. 1936). Eastern Front veteran; holder of the Knight's Cross. Prussian, cold, meticulous, fanatically possessive. A man who has confused appetite with ownership. His one tenderness: his younger sister **Ilse**, a nurse in Berlin. Confesses nothing, asks everything. Speaks to Marion in French; thinks in German; dreams in Russian.
- **Hauptmann Phillip Brandt**, 28. Half German, half nothing in particular: born in Warsaw, son of a German consular official and his wife; raised on Bagatela and in the Botanic Gardens; conscripted 1939; twice wounded; now *Ordonnanzoffizier* to von Reuth — the man who carries the Colonel's papers, keys and secrets. Speaks Polish like a native and has been hiding it. Keeper of Krystyna's adolescence: the boy who lent her books, who wrote her a letter in 1939 that he never sent. In Warsaw he recognises her in the first three minutes and says nothing for eleven days.
- **SS-Hauptsturmführer Werner Kroll**, 41. SD, Warsaw. A policeman's policeman: patient, courteous, terrifying. Hunting a "Frenchwoman" for much of the book without knowing that he is hunting his own Colonel's mistress. Keeps a card index of every human being he has ever shaken hands with.
- **Oberleutnant Dietrich Ehlers**, 34 — von Reuth's driver, loyal, in love with a German girl in Poznań. **Feldwebel Walter Kleist**, 47 — the Colonel's orderly; photographed his own children in a borrowed uniform. **Anna and Piotr Zaleski** — the Polish couple who keep the Warsaw house; they think Marion is a whore and she needs them to keep thinking it.
- **René Tissier**, 50 — Paris. Editor of a collaborationist weekly, collector of other men's women. Notices Marion. Survives the war in Paris and turns up in Warsaw in 1944 as a liaison for the SD. The thread that ties both cities together.

### The Underground
- **"Lis" (Fox)** — Jan Barczyk, 46, Krystyna's Paris handler. A former Warsaw bookseller with a bad heart and the best hands for a dead drop in Europe. Captured in March 1943; killed resisting.
- **"Kruk" (Raven)** — Colonel Stefan Wilczek, 52, AK, Warsaw district intelligence. Old enough to remember her father in his lovely ruined suit. Suspicious of Wesper, dependent on her, and finally the man who dies covering her escape.
- **Odile Marchand**, 30 — Paris. Milliner on the rue Cambon; sells hats to German wives; sews messages into hatbands. Laughs at everything until she doesn't.
- **Bolek Stankiewicz**, 17 — Krystyna's brother; disappeared into the underground ("Gray Ranks") in 1942; the ache in her body.
- **Professor Tadeusz Stankiewicz** — her father. Dying of stomach cancer in the wreck of the family apartment in Żoliborz; still correcting the proofs of a book that will never be printed.
- **Countess Jadwiga Ostrowska**, 68 — her mother's world, and the only person left alive who has seen Krystyna in a debutante's white dress. Recognises her in 1943 and chooses to say nothing. Becomes — without either woman ever admitting it — the spine of her safety in the city.
- **Pelagia**, the family's old maid, half-blind, keeps the portrait of Krystyna's mother in her coat.
- **Wiktor Groński**, 31 — the suitor she once pitied, now a "blue policeman" and a man who trades names. Recognises her at the Kercelak market and wants to own the secret.
- **The Quiet Ones** — *cichociemni*, agents dropped from England; one lands in the Kampinos Forest in the winter of 1943 with a suitcase radio, a cyanide capsule and orders that change everything.

## 4. Historical furniture (kept accurate)

- **Abwehrstelle Paris** at the Hôtel Majestic, 19 avenue Kléber; requisitioned apartments on the avenue Henri-Martin and the rue Rembrandt; the SD on the rue des Saussaies; the Vichy-collaborationist press.
- **Warsaw, 1943–44:** the Generalgouvernement; the *Standortkommandantur*; the destroyed ghetto and the wall; the aleja Szucha (Gestapo headquarters, where you entered through the gate and did not come out); the Pawiak prison on Dzielna; the *łapanka* round-ups; the blue police; *Kennkarte* identity cards, *Ausweis* work papers; the "Nur für Deutsche" tram cars; the black-market stalls behind the Zieleniak; ration coupons; *bimber* and chicory coffee; the curfew at 20:00; the lottery of street executions put up on placards; the Kampinos Forest; the Vistula and the Poniatowski Bridge; the German strategy of *Festung Warschau*.
- **The AK:** cells of five; chalk marks; the Gray Ranks; the Arsenal action, 26 March 1943; sabotage of the railway by *Kedyw*; Operation Tempest being argued out in drawing rooms long before it is fought; the *cichociemni*; "the silent unseen".
- Nothing is invented where a real date will do the work better; no real historical personage appears on the page.

## 5. Three engines (must run in every chapter)

1. **The occupation engine (plot):** every chapter advances the intelligence, the noose, or the rising. Titles of files: *Richtlinien für die Verteidigung Warschaus*; the *Verdächtigenlisten*; **Fall "Wotan"** — the plan for the Vistula line.
2. **The bed engine (carnal power):** every chapter contains the Colonel's hunger and her answer to it. The rule: his scenes are *dark, raw, bruising, proprietary*; the language is concrete, physical, unsentimental, never coy and never purple. She is never a passive victim in these scenes and never pretends to enjoy what she does not; she works. The reader must always feel both the horror and her competence.
3. **The love engine (Phillip):** slow, luminous, restrained. Hands, breath, rooms, memory. The consummation is late (Ch. 19) and terrifyingly tender — the exact opposite of everything in the house on Nowy Świat. He says her real name. That is the whole book in two words.

**Style rules:**
- Concrete nouns, short declaratives, deep interiority. No adverbs of manner in action beats. No "little did she know".
- French and German words are used sparingly, untranslated where clear from context. Polish names carry their diacritics.
- Time markers in Roman numeral dates at chapter openings are *not* used; dates arrive through weather, food, and politics.
- Every chapter ends on a turn: a door opening, a name said aloud, a decision taken.

## 7. Continuity ledger (as drafted)

**The woman.** Born 3 April 1917 in Warsaw; 26 in Paris 1943, 27 in Warsaw 1944, 34 in 1951. Cover birthday 9 April 1919 (Strasbourg) — a date that is also Anneliese von Reuth's birthday (ch.19). Four rules learned in Kraków, 1940; **Rule Four** ("never love") is stated (ch.02), quoted (ch.11), broken in silence (ch.19, the laundry), named and buried (ch.23, the bedroom). Amber necklace (mother's) → out of the flat Dec. 1939 with Pelagia → to the Countess → Zofia → back to her in 1943 (ch.13, twenty-eight beads) → worn to the end.

**The safe.** Organisation Todt pattern 1939, Gebr. Möller, Berlin SO 36, Nr. 44117; combination **9-4-36** = the ninth of April 1936 (Anneliese's date). Combination read off a procurement table in the office of the district and carried in her head from the first week in Warsaw (ch.08); spoken aloud by von Reuth in the dark, December 1943 (ch.13); opened 20 July 1944 (ch.19); the empty box found in the ruins 19 Jan. 1945 (epilogue).

**The papers.** Fall **"Wotan"** folder (five sheets, map of the Vistula line, rolling-stock table, order of the 9th Army, 30 June 1944), extracted from the safe 20 July 1944 (ch.19); carried into the laundry behind the boiler; taken over the wall in the machine-gun fire 7 Aug. 1944 (ch.24); the boy of twenty (Janek, Praga, bandaged right hand) killed with it in the Przejazd; the folder in the hands of the rising's district the next day (ch.25, confirmed by the woman of the Mokotów in the kitchen). The line-hold date: **8 August 1944** (ch.17, the "Wotan" page read aloud in the room above the furier's). Page of the Sicherheitsdienst card index (eleven surnames, four ticks, *Zaleski*) out of the safe sheet (ch.19) → floor of the laundry (ch.24) → recovered in the wet, 4 Oct. 1944 → tin box → the flat on the Solec (epilogue). Minox (Riga pattern) load film no. 12, sewn-in sleeve (ch.06) → seat back in a hatbox (ch.08) → bag lining (ch.19) → the tin box (epilogue).

**The card.** *Herschlag, Marion. Geb. 9.4.1919 in Straßburg*, with *Brauchbar. Prüfen im Sommer* in pencil on the back (ch.18); read out to her by Tissier in the photographer's shop (ch.21); taken out of the SD index by von Reuth and kept in the safe with Ilse's letters (ch.19); in the tin box after the war (epilogue).

**The question mark.** A pencil question mark against a name on a November 1942 report from the Militärbefehlshaber Paris, made by a Hauptsturmführer of the Paris SD (ch.04, via Odile's hatband paper); it travels with the file to Warsaw (ch.21, the Szucha scene); by Aug. 1944 the man who drew it is dead of his own courage (ch.25, the Kroll interview: "I know the shape of it").

**Kroll.** Card index and the green notebook; the 4 checks on her file, all clean (ch.21); the recruitment conversation and her negotiated terms (ch.18); *Prüfen im Sommer* (ch.18); the burned index in the Szucha courtyard (ch.22); the Pawiak emptied (ch.22); Kroll's own death in the pocket of a cellar in Breslau, April 1945 (ch.25).

**The house on the Mokotowska.** Anna and Piotr Zaleski. The cousin in the Gęsiówka; Anna's one question in the kitchen and her "no" (ch.11); the white cloth in the courtyard (ch.10); Piotr dead in a shelling of the Praga, 14 Sept. 1944; Anna found in 1946 on the Brzeska — the account closed at a table over a glass of water (epilogue).

**The household of the Bagatela.** Ehlers (driver, 34; Marta of Posen, whose street ceases to exist 28 July 1944; the photograph in the map case; dead at the well, 20 Aug. 1944 — ch.25); the cook from Bydgoszcz (sent out with money and a letter, 1 Aug. 1944 — ch.22); the furier of the staircase; the boy lieutenant from Kassel (ch.23-25).

**Others.** Zofia (the side gate, the amber, the Krasińskiego addresses); Pelagia (half-blind, the mother's portrait in her coat); the priest of the Visitation (the paper channel from Nov. 1943; *keep the woman*; dead in the cellar of the house, 29 Aug. 1944 — ch.20); the Countess Jadwiga Ostrowska (recognised at a tram stop looking at a paper flower, ch.10; the four gifts and the rules; fifty pages out of the Krasiński Library hidden in the wardrobe of a house on the Bagatela and sewn into the lining of the winter coat, ch.13; dead in the cellar of the Visitation, 29 Aug. 1944 — ch.25); the priest's orders; Bolek (the file, the "unentbehrlich" stamp, ch.12; the scar, the wedding, epilogue); the photographer Wierzbicki, Odile Marchand (the last hatband, ch.04; gone to Lyon), Lis (Feb.-Mar. 1943, ch.03-04).

**The lover.** Phillip Brandt, born 1915, Warsaw; Sept. 1939 Modlin, the broken wrist, the German major's coffee (ch.12); recognised her in a corridor of the Hôtel Majestic (ch.03-04); the mathematics textbook on the piano (ch.11); the seven seconds in the corridor; the pistol shot into the yard to explain her crossing (ch.24); reading her real name aloud in the ruin, 19 Jan. 1945 (epilogue); the modest bicycle, the tin box of English biscuits, the two-room flat on the Solec.

**The plan dates.** Rising hour: 17:00, 1 Aug. 1944 (learnt from a child's skipping song, ch.20). Last civil-administration train: 31 July 1944, 05:30 (ch.22) — she refuses it on the platform. Wife/family train of the district: 28 June 1944 (ch.13, ch.18). von Reuth leaves the house for good the morning of 2 Sept. 1944; the rising capitulates 2 Oct. 1944; the city empties through Pruszków; the Red Army enters the ruins 17 Jan. 1945 (ch.25, epilogue).

**Real furniture used.** aleja Szucha 25 (SD, the crematorium smoke over the city, 31 July 1944), the Pawiak on the Dzielna, the Blue Palace on the Ujazdowskie, the Krasiński Library (burned 30 Aug. 1944), the church of the Visitation (fired 29 Aug. 1944), the Pruszków transit camp, the Wola killing of 5-7 Aug. 1944, the Kercelak market, the ghetto wall and its razing, the Vistula ice road, the 20 July 1944 attempt on Hitler (ch.19).

# Chapter 25: August

The case lay in the middle of the street of the Przejazd for two days and two nights, and the dead boy lay on top of it.

In the daytime the sun stood in the street from ten o'clock until four, and there was a smell in that quarter that everybody in the house on the Bagatela knew, and at night the machine gun on the roof of the building of the insurance company swept the mouth of the passage with two bursts an hour, so that no living person came near the middle of that street from any direction; and the Poles on the roofs of the buildings at the corner of the Novum watched the street through the daytime with their sights and did not fire at the boy, because a man does not fire at his own dead; and the German gun crew on the roof of the insurance company watched the street through the daytime with a pair of field glasses and did not fire at the boy either, because the men of the third company of the garrison had been in the street on the morning of the seventh of August at half past four and had seen who the boy was and what was under him, and there was not one of them who had said a word about it to the officers.

She went out for him at half past four in the morning of the ninth of August.

There was no plan of any kind in it, and there was no cover: she came down the back stairs of the house of the General of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw in a dark dress and a pair of shoes with no heels, and past the men of the garrison sleeping in the passage, and out through the garden door into the yard, and through the wall on the southern side at the place where the brick course was broken, which was the way she had gone on the seventh; and then she was in the yard of the building in the next street, and the yard was a heap of the burned house, and a strip of the first light of the morning was standing in the ash of it; and she got through the yard and into the back of the passage of the Przejazd, and stood in the mouth of it with the street eleven metres in front of her.

The boy was on the pavement in the middle of the street with the case under his body.

She walked out.

There was nothing in the city of Warsaw at that hour: no firing in that quarter for the first time in eight days; no aeroplane; no traffic; the light coming up grey along the roofs and the walls of a street of a European capital with the windows out of it and the plaster on the roadway; and a woman of twenty-seven in a dark dress walking the way from the mouth of the passage to the middle of the street at the hour of half past four in the morning, in the light of the beginning of the day.

She knelt down.

The boy was nineteen or twenty, and he had been dead for two days in an August, and she had known him since the winter of 1943, and his name was Janek, and she had never once in the time known his surname, and it was in a pocket of his coat, in a Kennkarte, with his photograph in it and a rubber stamp of the district of Warsaw, and she took that out of the coat and put it in the pocket of her dress, because there was a mother somewhere in this city of a million people who was going to want to know where her son was, and there was going to be nobody in the world who was going to be able to tell her, and a piece of paper in the pocket of a woman who did not exist was better than nothing at all.

Then she lifted the body onto her knee, the way a person lifts a sleeping child, and got the case out from under it, and the case was wet through with the rain of the eighth of August and heavy and dark with the water, and she put it on the ground beside her, and laid the boy back down; and then she did a thing that was not in any arithmetic anywhere, and it was that she took his cap off the top of his head and put it over his face, because the sun had been on him for two days and he was a boy of twenty who had come out of a chemist's shop in the Praga district and had stood in a doorway with a bandage on his right arm and a case in his left hand, and because there was nobody left in the city of Warsaw to do it.

And while she was doing that, in the grey light, up on the roof of the building of the insurance company on the Polna, a German of the third company of the garrison of the district of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw was standing behind the sandbags with his hands in his pockets and a cigarette he was not permitted to smoke in his mouth, and he watched the woman of the household of the General of the district of the intelligence service kneel in the middle of the street of the Przejazd at half past four in the morning and take a case from under a dead Pole and put a cap over his face; and the man watched it and said nothing at all, and afterwards he never once told anybody in the war, and it was one of the eleven reasons that the woman was never arrested in the four years of the German administration of the city of Warsaw.

She carried the case back through the ashes of the yard and over the wall and in through the door of the garden, and it weighed four or five kilos.

Phillip Brandt was standing on the path of the garden under the walnut tree when she came through the gate, in a tunic and no cap with a cup in his hand, and he had been standing there for a quarter of an hour because he had gone into the room on the first floor at four o'clock and the bed had been empty and there had been nobody in the house to ask.

He did not say anything. He took the case out of her hand and set it on the bench, and looked at the street beyond the wall and then at her hands and then at her face.

"It is wet," she said. "The papers are inside a waxed envelope, the way the district's papers are, and the envelope was cut by me and sealed by me with a candle in the laundry on the thirty-first of July, and if the paper inside the envelope is intact, then the plan of the Fall **Wotan** of the district of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw is in the hands of a woman of the household of the General of the district of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw, and there is not one man in the army of Germany who is ever going to know that."

"You walked into the street."

"Yes."

"Krystyna."

"I put a cap over his face," she said. "That is what I have to say about it. Not the case. The cap."

And Phillip Brandt, captain of the intelligence section of the district of Warsaw, in the garden of the house of a German general, at twenty minutes to five in the morning on the ninth of August 1944, stood with his weight on his heels and looked at the grey sky over the roofs of the city of her birth and said, in Polish, in the ordinary voice of a man saying the time:

"There is a woman in the house of the furier's who came through the wall on the third with a bag of potatoes and who has been in the kitchen since. She goes out through the garden wall at half past five in the morning to the yard of the church of the Visitants with the linen of the household in a basket; she has done it every day for six days, and the men at the gate of the garden have stopped looking in the basket on the fourth; and she is a woman of sixty-one from the district of the Mokotów who has been washing the linen of the German houses of this city for four years, and there is nobody in the German administration of Warsaw who has ever looked at her for more than a second in the time." He picked the case up off the bench. "Give me the waxed envelope."

She gave it to him, and he put it inside his tunic, against his ribs, where a captain's tunic is buttoned to the throat.

"I am going to the kitchen now," he said, "and I am going to stand in the door of the kitchen with the men of the second squad at the table and I am going to ask the woman of the Mokotów for a cup of coffee, and I am going to say, in Polish, in front of the room, the thing that a Pole says to a woman of his mother's generation at the end of a night, and it is going to be: *There was a paper in the pocket of the tunic of the captain of the ordnance, and it is a paper of the district and it belongs in the basket.* And she is going to look at my face for four seconds, because she is a woman of sixty-one who washes the linen of the German administration of this city, and then she is going to take it out of the yard at half past five in the basket with the linen of an officer of the Generalgouvernement in it, and from the church of the Visitants it is going to be in the hands of the district of the rising in the day, and there is going to be nobody able to say a word about it, ever, to anybody at all."

"It is four hundred metres through the German quarter," she said.

"It is four hundred metres through the German quarter," said Phillip Brandt, "and it is in the basket of a woman of sixty-one with the linen of the German houses of the city of Warsaw, and there is not one sentry of the Wehrmacht of the district who is going to open that basket on the ninth of August 1944, because there is a war on in the street, and because the men of the army of Germany have a mother, and I have been in that army for four years, and I am the only man in it who is able to tell you the truth about a German sentry: that he is able to shoot a boy of fifteen in the middle of a street in the middle of the day and he is not able to look in the basket of the woman who washes his shirt."

---

The basket went out at half past five, and the woman of the Mokotów came back at eleven with the linen of the church and the potatoes for the third company of the garrison, and she stood in the kitchen of the house on the Bagatela and put a bag of onions on the table and looked at the woman of the household of the General of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw for four seconds, and said, in Polish, in the voice of a woman of sixty-one who has been washing the linen of a German city for four years:

"The Frau of the household should have a coat. The nights are getting cold, and there is not one woman in the quarter of the Mokotów who is going to be able to sleep in a house above the ground after the twentieth of August."

And she went out into the yard with the basin of the linen and hung it up on the line, in the middle of the hammering of the machine gun of the corner of the Polna, and that was the entire ceremony of the receipt of the intelligence which had cost the life of a boy of twenty from the Praga district and the first part of the life of a woman of the household of a German general.

---

On the twelfth of August the Sicherheitsdienst came for her.

They came at eight in the evening in the same Citroën with three men in it and a driver, and the Hauptsturmführer of forty-five with the face like a retired schoolmaster came up the steps of the house on the Bagatela in the middle of the shelling and stood in the hall with his cap in his hand and read a paper to the house in a loud, flat, courteous voice: that the person of Marion Herschlag, French citizen, of the household of the General of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw, was to be brought to the command post of the Governor at the Bruhl Palace for questioning on the matter of the security of the district, and that the order was of the Sicherheitsdienst of the district of Warsaw, and that the person was to be handed over at once.

The boy-lieutenant from Kassel stood at the bottom of the marble stair with the paper in his hand and did not know what to say to it, because there is nothing in the regulations of the army of Germany that tells a lieutenant of twenty-three what to do when a Hauptsturmführer of the Sicherheitsdienst hands him a warrant for the household of his own general; and he was standing there with it in his hand and his mouth open when the front door of the house opened again, and the General of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw came in off the step, out of the street, with the dust of the Blue Palace on his shoulders and his service pistol still in the holster.

He had come from the Palace on the telephone call of a soldier of the guard of the house, and he had driven four hundred metres through the machine-gun fire of the corner of the Krucza in a Mercedes with a hole in the windscreen, and he had done it in the middle of an evening of the rising for one reason, and he said the reason out loud in the marble hall in the flats of that house at eleven minutes past eight in the evening of the twelfth of August 1944, in front of the men of the third company of the garrison and the boy lieutenant and a woman of the household and a Hauptsturmführer of the Sicherheitsdienst of the district of Warsaw:

"Herr Hauptsturmführer. You have come into a house of the Wehrmacht of the district of Warsaw in the middle of the defensive zone of the line of the Bagatela in the middle of the afternoon orders of the day of the district of the command, without an escort, with three men, in a French car with the paint of the civil administration on the door, and you have read a paper to a lieutenant of the reserve of the third company of the third battalion of the garrison in the middle of a hall with his men in it. There are four words for that in the order of the day of this district and one of them is *Befehlsverweigerung*, and I have the text of the order of the day of the district on the table in the study."

"General, there is a warrant—"

"There is a warrant of the Sicherheitsdienst of the district for the person of a woman of the household of an officer of the Wehrmacht of the district of Warsaw," said Friedrich von Reuth, "and there is a an order of the district of the command of the tenth of August 1944 concerning the persons attached to the staff of the district in the period of the defence, and it is in the file of the district in my own hand, and the order says that a person attached to the staff of the district of the intelligence service is not to be removed from the defensive sector by any office of the administration of the district of Warsaw without the signature of the commanding general of the sector. Where is the signature of the commanding general of the sector of the Bagatela?"

The Hauptsturmführer of the Sicherheitsdienst stood with his cap in his hand in the middle of the marble hall of the house on the Bagatela and did not say anything for four seconds; and in the four seconds of that silence there were seven hundred thousand people in the city of Warsaw on the other side of the wall, and the quarter was going up in a smoke of a week of burning, and the war of the east was going to be over in eight months, and there was not one man in that hall who was going to be alive in the year 1950 except two.

"There is not a signature," he said.

"Then go out of this house," said the General of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw, "and come back with the signature of the commanding general of the sector of the line of the Bagatela, and if the commanding general of the sector is not able to be found in this city in the next four months, then the warrant is going to have to wait for the peace, and the peace is going to be a long time."

The Hauptsturmführer put his cap on.

"Frau Herschlag," he said, to the woman at the bottom of the marble stair, over the shoulder of the general, in the flat voice of a man of forty-five in the criminal police of the city of Breslau: "I am going to say one sentence to you, and then I am going to go out of this door and I am never going to be in this house again in the war. There is a question mark in a file in the administration of the district of Warsaw, and it is beside a sentence in a report of the department of the defence of the Reich of the district of the army of Paris, and the question mark is going to be in the file of the district on the day that the district is destroyed, and the paper of the district is going to be in a lorry going west for the next eleven months, and there is not one man in the criminal police of Germany who is going to be able to make the question mark into a name, because there is not a single Pole in the city of Warsaw who is ever going to say what he knows to the administration of Germany, and there is not one woman in the Reich who would be believed if she did." He looked at her. "But I want you to know, madame, on the twelfth of August 1944, in the hall of the house of the general, that I know the shape of it, and that the shape of it has been in my file since May, and that the reason that I am going out of this door without the woman of the household of the General of the district of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw is not the order of the day of the district." He put the cap on his head with both of his hands. "It is the general. In my life in the police, madame, I have met exactly one kind of man who is not able to be worked on, and it is not the fanatic and it is not the honest one, and it is the man who is in love, because that man has put the object of his life outside of the world, and no police in the history of Europe has ever been able to get at a thing that is in a place that is not in the world."

He went out through the front door of the house of the General of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw at twenty-two minutes past eight in the evening of the twelfth of August 1944, and the Citroën of the civil administration of the district went down the street of the Bagatela and turned in the direction of the Saxon Garden, and the car was hit twice on the way by the fire of a machine gun from the roof of the building at the corner of the Krucza, and the Hauptsturmführer of forty-five with the face like a retired schoolmaster of the criminal police of the city of Breslau was in the front seat of it with a hole in his left hand, and he was in Silesia in October 1944 with the administration of the district of Warsaw, and he was in Breslau in February 1945 with a wife and two of three of the children, and he was in a cellar in the southern part of the city of Breslau in the middle of April 1945 when the city was in the fire of the army of the Soviet Union, and there is not one paper in the world with his name on it after the nineteenth of April 1945, and there never will be.

---

Ehlers died on the twentieth of August, at half past three in the afternoon, at the well in the yard of the building behind the house.

He had gone out with a bucket and the second man, because there was no water in the pipes of the quarter since the fifteenth and the men of the third company were taking the household's water from the courtyard of a Polish building behind the garden wall; and there was not one thing about it that was different from the eleven days before it; and a mortar of the Polish battery in the district of the Śródmieście, which had been ranging on the yard for the morning and had been three hundred metres short three times, put a shell into the middle of the well at half past three, and the man of the second squad behind him was in the passage of the building with the bucket still in his hand and the shrapnel of the shell in his ear and lived, and the driver of the General of the district of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw, of thirty-four years of age, of the town of Posen, with a photograph of a girl who did not have a street in the flap of his map case, died in the yard of a building in the quarter of the Mokotów of the city of Warsaw on the twentieth of August 1944 at the age of thirty-four years, and it took four minutes.

They brought the map case to the house.

It was on the table in the hall of the drawing room of the Countess Jadwiga Ostrowska for the afternoon with the blood of the man on the leather; and the boy-lieutenant of twenty-three from the town of Kassel stood in front of the table and opened it with the greatest care and took the papers of the district out of it and put them on the table, and last of all there was a small photograph with the corners of it worn round by the hand of the man, of a girl of about twenty in the long coat of the winter of the year 1942 with a bicycle and a house of the suburb of the town of Posen behind her; and the lieutenant of twenty-three from the town of Kassel looked at it for a long time in the middle of the afternoon with the men of his own squad at the table, and then he put it face down on the table and went out of the room and said nothing at all.

Krystyna Stankiewiczówna took the photograph off the table at half past eight in the evening, when the hall was empty, and put it in the pocket of the lining of her bag, where the Minox had been, and it was in a bag in the hands of a woman of the household of the General of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw for the rest of the war, and it crossed the Vistula in the pocket of a Polish woman in the October of 1944, and it was in a tin box under a floor in a house in the province of the Łódź in the January of 1945, and it was in a flat in the district of the Praga of the city of Warsaw in the year 1946, with a bicycle and a house of a suburb of the town of Posen and a girl of the age of twenty in a long coat of the winter of 1942 in it, and there was never one word said about it in the life of the two people who kept it, and the photograph is in a drawer of a flat of a city in Poland at this hour.

The General left Warsaw on the second of September 1944.

The order came on the twenty-ninth of August: the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw was to be withdrawn to the west with the records of the district in the first week of September and re-formed in Silesia, and there was a paper with the seal of the army group of the south on it, and it was on the table of the study of the house on the Bagatela on the afternoon of the thirtieth of August, and the man who read it was in the drawing room of the house afterwards for an hour and a half with the door shut.

He came out at eight in the evening and said: "Come up."

---

He had his service pistol and his belt on the bed and a paper of the district on the table under the lamp, and the room was in the heat of the last days of an August in a city in the fifth year of a war, and he stood at the window behind the blackout blind with his back to the room the way he had stood in a hundred rooms in two years, and he said:

"There is a train on the fifth. The the office of the district, the files of the office, the families of the staff that are in this city, and the records of the war of the district of Warsaw. It goes to the west by way of Skierniewice and Łódź, and it is a train of the army, and it is going to get through, because there is not one thing that a Polish rising is able to do about a train in the open country." He turned around. "There is a place on that train for a woman of the household of the General of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw, and there is not a paper with my name on it, and there is not a register, and there is not a stamp, and if you are on that train on the fifth of September at half past five in the morning, then there is nothing in the world that anybody in the administration of Germany is ever going to be able to say about it." He put his the hand on the paper on the table. "And there is a second thing, and it is the only thing I have ever asked of a human being in the war, and I am going to ask it now, at nine o'clock in the evening of the thirtieth of August 1944, in this room, and I am going to ask it in the ordinary voice of a man who has not slept in a month."

"Ask it."

"There is a house in the Pomerania," said Friedrich von Reuth, "with a garden and a walnut tree and eleven rooms and a cook of seventy years of the age and a photograph on the wall of the study of a woman in a garden in the year 1931 with a dog at her the feet; and there is a sister in Berlin of the age of sixty-one and a bank in the city of Breslau with eleven thousand marks of the money of the family of the house; and the house has been in the family of my mother since the year 1784, and there is not one member of the family in it since the year 1911, and there is a room at the top of the stairs of it with a window on the garden, and I have been in it for the last four years of the war in my own head at the end of every day of the war, and there is a woman in that room in the year 1945 in a chair by the window with a lamp turned low, and that is the house and the garden and the war for the rest of my life, and the woman in the room has a face that I have been looking at for two years in the city of Warsaw in the middle of the German administration of a country that is not her country, and the name of the woman is Marion Herschlag, of the city of Strasbourg."

She was standing in the middle of the room of the first floor of the house on the Bagatela, at nine o'clock in the evening of the thirtieth of August 1944, in the last light of a Polish August, in a grey dress with her hair up, with a bag with a Minox and a photograph of a girl with a bicycle in the lining of it and a card with her name on it and a pencil sentence on the back of it, and the girl in a village near Posen in the year 1942 did not have a house or a street or a bicycle in the world any more, and a boy of twenty from the Praga district was in the middle of the street of the Przejazd with his cap over his face and a Kennkarte in the pocket of a woman who did not exist, and a woman of twenty-seven of the city of Warsaw stood in a German house in the middle of a burning city and looked at a man of fifty-five who had refused to put his name on a paper of the SS and the police of the district of Warsaw, and who had put his career and his life and his honour between a warrant of the Sicherheitsdienst and a woman of the household of his house.

And she said: "Yes."

"Say it again."

"Yes," she said. "On the fifth of September, at half past five in the morning, at the station, with a coat and a case, and the house in the Pomerania with the walnut tree and the room at the top of the stairs with the window on the garden, and the woman in the chair by the window with the lamp turned low; and a man of fifty-five who is going to be in the hall of it with the mud of the war on his boots, and who is going to be a person in a house of the year 1784 and not a general of the army of Germany, and there is not going to be a single day of the rest of the war in which one of the two of us is going to be in a room without the other."

He crossed the room and took her face in both his hands, and looked at her for a long time from a distance of eleven centimetres, and his hands were shaking, and he was fifty-five years of age, and he said the last sentence that he said to her in the four years of the arrangement:

"You are the only thing I have ever had that the war was not able to take."

He went out of the house on the Bagatela at half past six in the morning of the second of September, and he went down the marble stair with his cap and his case and the paper of the district under his arm, and the house was standing at the bottom of the stair, the boy lieutenant of twenty-three from the town of Kassel and the men of the third company of the garrison of the house on the Bagatela, and the car was at the door with the hole in the windscreen of it mended with a plate of the tin of a ration box and a driver of the third company at the wheel, and the General of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw stopped at the bottom of the stair in front of the men of the garrison of the house of the General of the district of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw, and he said one sentence, in German, in the ordinary voice of a general of the army of Germany:

"Die Frau Generalin bleibt im Haus. Sie steht unter meinem Befehl."

*The Frau Generalin stays in the house. She is under my command.*

And the woman of the household of the General of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw stood in the middle of the marble hall of the house on the Bagatela at half past six in the morning of the second of September 1944 and watched the car going out of the gate of the garden and turning in the direction of the avenue of the Ujazdowskie, and she did not cry, and she did not move, and she was in the middle of the fifth year of the war.

---

The city died in the thirty days of the September.

It was not a battle any more by the middle of the month: it was a machine; and the machine had eleven parts, and the parts were in the files of the office of the Governor of the district of Warsaw, and the papers of it were captured in the year 1945 and are in a an archive, and the things that are in the papers of them are:

The burnings, which were done by the formations of the police and the SS and the men of the battalions of the eastern formations of the army of Germany in the streets of the city of Warsaw from the fourth of August to the second of October 1944, in the ordinary way of a work of a day, and there is not one man in the history of the war who has ever been able to say the number of the houses of it, and the figure that the office of the Governor used in the November of 1944 in a report to Berlin was 84 per cent of the buildings of the city of Warsaw.

The library of the Krasiński, which was burned on the thirtieth of August with the manuscripts of the Polish nation in it, and the archive of the city, and the palace of the Krasinski in the square of the Bank, and the church of the Visitation of the Blessed Virgin Mary of the city of Warsaw on the twenty-ninth of August 1944 with the cellar of it in which there were eleven women of the age of sixty years and a superior of the age of seventy and a woman of the city of Warsaw of the age of sixty-nine years whose name was the Countess Jadwiga Ostrowska, and there is not one paper in the world about the death of that woman and there never will be, and the trace of her life is a photograph in a drawer of a flat of the city of Warsaw and a telegram of the year 1931 from a man in the cavalry of the army of Poland.

And the Polish people of the city of Warsaw, of whom there were a million in the month of the July of 1944, went out of the city of Warsaw in the month of the October of 1944 in the columns of the roads of the west, with the cases and the bundles and the prams and the old people and the children, and them were in the transit camp of the town of Pruszków of the district of Warsaw, and them were sorted by the men of the administration of Germany into three groups: the men of the age of the work, who went to the labour camps of the Reich, and the old people and the women who did not work, who were sent to the province of the west of the Generalgouvernement, and the children, who were not sorted at all.

The house on the Bagatela was burned on the first of October 1944.

The third company of the garrison of the district of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw had gone to the west in the middle of the September, and the house was standing empty in the middle of the German quarter of the city with the furniture of the drawing room of the Countess Jadwiga Ostrowska in it, and the men of the battalion of the something, who were in the quarter in the last days of the rising, put a fire into the drawing room of the first floor of the house in the afternoon of the first of October, and the fire went up the stair and into the room on the first floor with a bed in it and a wardrobe of a winter of 1941 in it, and into the study of the ground floor with the safe of the pattern of the Organisation Todt of the year 1939 in the wall of it with the dial of the brass and the plate of the serial number of it under the dial, and there was not one man of the city of Warsaw who ever knew in the war the number of the combination.

---

On the second of October 1944, at eight o'clock in the evening, the firing in the city of Warsaw stopped.

It stopped along the Vistula and up the avenue of the Ujazdowskie and in the quarter of the Mokotów and in the district of the Żoliborz and in the district of the Praga on the other side of the river; and the city of a million people went quiet, in the middle of the ash of October; and the men of the army of the rising came up out of the cellars and the sewers of the city, and them were in the hands of the army of Germany by the middle of the month, and them were prisoners of war of the Reich in the camps of the west, and there were a 15,000 of them, and there was not one of them who was not in a war of six years; and the men of the army of Germany began in the month of the October of 1944 the systematic destruction of what was left of the city of Warsaw, which had been the third capital of the Europe of the year 1938.

And a woman of the household of the General of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw stood in the middle of a quarter of the city of her birth at eight o'clock in the evening of the second of October 1944 and listened to the silence of a city of a million people in the middle of the fire, and the silence was worse than the noise of the two months of the rising, and there was not one person alive in the world to whom she was able to say the thing that she was thinking, and the thing that she was thinking was:

*They are going to come for me now. There is no German house left in this city to hide in, and there is no army of Germany in the middle of it to be the shadow, and there is a card with my name on it in a file in a lorry on a road to the west, and there is a photograph of a man of fifty-five with a woman of the Pomerania in a house with a walnut tree in the garden of it, and there is a girl in a village near the town of Posen whose bicycle is in the pocket of my bag, and there is a boy of twenty in the middle of the street of the Przejazd with a his own cap on his face, and there is a city of a million people in the columns of the roads of the west, and there is a thing that I have to do before the end of the world and it is going to have to be done in the next four days and there is not a single person in the city of Warsaw who is able to be told it.*

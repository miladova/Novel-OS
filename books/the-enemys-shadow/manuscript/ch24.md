# Chapter 24: The Wall of Names

On the fifth of August 1944, in the district of the Wola of the city of Warsaw, the troops of the SS and police of the district, and the troops of the eastern formations of the army of the Reich, and the men of the battalion of the traitors of the Russian army who had been given to the German command in the month of March, carried out the orders that had been written down in the district of the command on the first night of the Rising.

The order has a name in the history of the war, and the name is a German word, and it is on the paper in a file in a building that still exists, and Krystyna Stankiewiczówna never read the paper and never saw the signature; and the only thing that she ever knew of it, in the war, and the only thing she ever afterwards said of it, was what she saw from a roof on the third of August, and what Phillip Brandt told her in a passage in the house on the Bagatela in the four seconds in which the passage was empty.

The name of the district of the Wola is on no document in this book, and two hundred thousand people of the city of Warsaw were taken out of it in four days, and there are eleven streets in that district that no longer have a name; and on the table of the office of the district of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw at the Blue Palace, in that week, the reports of the district came in every hour on the hour, and them were filed, and the file has a number, and the number was written in a hand that Krystyna Stankiewiczówna knew; and in those four days the woman of the household of the General of the intelligence of the district of Warsaw sat in the chair by the window of the small room of the house on the Bagatela, in a house full of the men of the garrison, and did the arithmetic of a piece of paper that had a signature on it that was not there.

---

At half past nine in the morning of the sixth of August, a party of the Sicherheitsdienst came to the house.

There were five of them and a car, and the car was a French one, a Citroën of 1938 with the mark of the district painted on the door in white; and the officer of them was a Hauptsturmführer of about forty-five with a face like a retired schoolmaster and a hand that never stopped moving over his own chin; and he came up the steps of the house on the Bagatela in the middle of the morning and showed the boy-lieutenant from Kassel a paper, and stood in the marble hall and looked at it with the flat, tired attention of a man who had spent the week in the offices of the aleja Szucha that were now a burnt shell with a burnt index in the middle of it, and he said:

"I am to secure the papers of the district of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw that are in the house of the General of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw, and I am to take them to the command post of the Governor, and I am to do it in the next hour, because at noon there is going to be nothing in this quarter of the city that is not under fire from the roofs of the buildings on the Krucza."

"Then you will want the study," said Krystyna Stankiewiczówna, from the bottom of the marble stair, in German, in the flat voice of the Frau of the household.

The Hauptsturmführer looked at her for a long moment.

"Frau Herschlag," he said. "Of the household of the General. French. Of Strasbourg. The employee of the administration of the district since 1942. The Frau is on the list of the persons attached to the staff of the district."

"I am on the list," she said.

"This is a very correct house," said the Hauptsturmführer, "and a very correct list, and in the last ten days I have read four hundred lists of the persons of the administration of this district, and every one of them is a list of a person who has gone to the west and left the office to the fire, and the Frau is the only person on any of the four hundred lists who is in the middle of a rising in the middle of the German quarter of the city of Warsaw." He took his cap off and polished the badge of it with a handkerchief. "The study, then."

They did it with a correctness that was worse than violence.

The four men of the party went over the study for forty minutes, and they took the registers of the district of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw off the shelves in the order in which the shelves were arranged, and they put them in the four iron cases that they had brought in the Citroën; and they were, all four of them, men who had been in the police of a German city before the war, and they did their work the way a bank does its work: nothing in the study was thrown, and nothing in it was broken, and every one of the four men took off his cap in the study of a General of the German army, which is a thing that a man of the criminal police of the Reich does out of an old and unbreakable respect that is one of the last things in the world that anybody in that house was able to recognise as human.

And at the end of the forty minutes the Hauptsturmführer stood in front of the safe.

It was open, because the office of the district had been in the study since the first night; and there was a folder in it with the deeds of the Pomeranian land, and a portfolio of forty-one sheets of paper with the watercolours of a dead woman in them, and eleven letters of a nurse of Berlin, and a brown envelope with a word on it in the hand of the General; and the Hauptsturmführer took the contents of the safe out of it and laid them on the writing table, and looked at the four bundles for a long time.

"Frau Herschlag."

"Yes."

"There is a small square of paper in the hand of a clerk of this office among these papers," said the Hauptsturmführer of the Sicherheitsdienst, "and it has eleven surnames of the population of this district on it, and four of the eleven have a tick against them, and it is a page out of the card index of the office of the Sicherheitsdienst in the aleja Szucha, and the card index of the aleja Szucha burned on the thirty-first of July in the courtyard of the building, and there is not one man left alive in German Warsaw who is able to say what that page was doing in the safe of a general of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw."

"It was there when I opened the safe this morning at nine," she said. "The the office of the district has used the study since the first of August, and there have been twenty-nine men of the garrison in this house since the evening of that day, and there is not one object in the safe of the General that has been in my hand since the thirty-first of July, and if the Hauptsturmführer wishes to know what the office of the district of the intelligence service has put in the safe of the General of the district of the intelligence service in the last week, then the Hauptsturmführer will have to be asking the office of the district of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw, which is at this hour in the Blue Palace with the staff of the district, and which is a building that is under the fire of the Poles from the roof of the house on the Krucza, and there is a hole in the hall of it since Tuesday morning."

He looked at her.

"You defend this house well, madame."

"I am the Frau of this house," she said.

"Then perhaps the Frau will permit me to remark," said the Hauptsturmführer of the Sicherheitsdienst, in the same flat, tired voice, "that a man of my office in the month of November 1942 read the report of a captain of the department of the defence of the Reich of the district of the army in Paris, concerning a shooting in the rue de Lille, and the report had a sentence in it about a woman of the establishment of the Hôtel Majestic who was at the scene and had said the name of a Polish family; and there was a question mark in the margin of that sentence, and the question mark was made by the hand of a Hauptsturmführer of the office of the Sicherheitsdienst of Paris who is at this hour in a building somewhere in the west, and I have known that question mark since the month of May, because it is in the file which my office has carried out of the aleja Szucha in four lorries in the last four days, and it is the only file of the district of Warsaw that we were not able to burn, and it is going to the command post of the Governor this afternoon with these papers."

He put his cap on.

"There is going to be an investigation of this district, madame. There is going to be an investigation of the intelligence service of this city, because there is a page in the safe of a general and a question mark in the file of a Hauptsturmführer and eleven thousand dead men in the streets of a district with four hundred thousand people in it, and there is not one man in the administration of this district who is not going to be asked the question before the month of October: *how did the Poles know?*" He stood at the door of the study with the four iron cases behind him and the four men of the party carrying them out through the hall, and he said the last thing that he said in the house on the Bagatela. "And a woman of the household of the staff of the district, who is the only person of the staff who is in our quarter in the middle of the rising, is going to be asked the question in a room with a table in it, and it is going to be a room on the first floor of the building of the Governor at the Bruhl Palace, and there is a chair in the room, and the chair has a strap on the left arm of it and no strap on the right, and the men who work in the room are men of the criminal police of Breslau, and there is not one of them who has ever looked at a woman's face."

He went out.

And in the marble hall, on the way past her, at the bottom of the stair, with the four iron cases going out of the front door, the Hauptsturmführer of the Sicherheitsdienst stopped, and looked up at the stair and the ceiling and the hall of the house, and he said, in the same voice:

"This is a good house. My mother had a house with a stair of this kind in Breslau in 1911, and there is a mirror of the same kind on the landing, and the house was in the possession of my family from the year of the 1890s, and there was a walnut tree in the garden." He put the cap on with both hands, and stood with the hall of a Polish general's house in his eyes. "I am going to be dead in the next four months, madame, and the house of my mother is going to be in the Russian zone, and there is not one object in the world that a man of forty-five in this war is able to keep, and the reason for this rising is on that table in the room with the strap." He shrugged. "Secure the coats in the wardrobe of the first floor," he said to the sergeant behind him. "There is going to be a winter."

---

They took the coats at eleven o'clock in the morning.

She was on the landing when they did it. She had come out of the small room and stood at the head of the back stairs with a tray of cups in her hands, because the cook had made coffee for the men of the party in the kitchen and the Frau of the household carried it herself; and she stood at the head of the stairs and looked into the open door of the room on the first floor and watched a sergeant of the Sicherheitsdienst of about thirty take from the wardrobe of her room the grey wool coat, and the brown coat of the winter of 1943, and the winter coat of the winter of 1941 with the parcel sewn into the lining of it, in which there were forty-one sheets of paper with the watercolours of a woman who had been dead for eleven years, and a lock of fair hair in a twist of tissue, and eleven letters of a nurse of Berlin, and a pass of the district of the intelligence service, and a thing that was not in the world any more.

She did not move.

She stood at the head of the back stairs of the house on the Bagatela on the sixth of August 1944 with a tray of coffee in her hands in front of four men of the criminal police of the Reich, and watched the coat of the winter of 1941 going down the marble stair of the house of the General of the intelligence of the district of Warsaw on the arm of a sergeant of thirty who was going to wear it to Warsaw in January 1945, if he was alive in January 1945; and she made a decision in those eleven seconds that she never afterwards wholly understood, and the decision was to do nothing at all.

Not the tray. Not a word. Not the very small, very reasonable sentence that a woman of the household of a General can say to a sergeant of the security service about a coat that was bought in the rue Saint-Honoré in the month of October 1941 and is the only object of her own property in the house; not the face; not the eyes.

She had run the four years on a single principle, and the principle was written nowhere and had been in her since the corridor of a house on the Mokotowska: *a woman of the household of a German General is permitted to defend a house, and she is not permitted to be attached to anything in it.* Attachment is the thing that the police of a country read on the face of a person before they read the file; and the amber of her mother's mother, and the forty-one sheets of Anneliese von Reuth, and the lock of fair hair, and the letters of Ilse, went down the marble stair of the house on the Bagatela in a sergeant's arms at eleven o'clock in the morning of the sixth of August 1944 while the woman of the household stood on the landing with a tray of coffee in her hands and an entirely ordinary face, and she did not say a word about it, and in the years afterwards she was never able to decide whether the thing that she had done on the landing had been the discipline of a professional or the last and deepest piece of the arithmetic of a woman who had been looking at that coat for four years and had known, in the spring, that there was never going to be any way to carry it out of the city.

---

The paper came over the wall at three o'clock in the morning of the seventh of August.

It came the way such things always come, which is with a man's voice and a stone: a pebble over the wall of the garden and a knock on the garden door that was the knock of a Pole, and she was awake, because in the last six nights she had not slept more than two hours; and she went down the back stairs in the dark past the men of the garrison sleeping in the passage with their boots on, and opened the garden door eleven centimetres, and outside, against the wall, in the dark, there was a boy's voice, in Polish, very fast:

"Frau. It is Janek. There is a paper in my hand, and it is from the command of the district, and the command is at this hour in the school on the Barokowa with forty men and a wireless set, and the command is going to be in the Old Town by the twelfth because the centre of the city is on fire, and there is a an order in the paper and it is for the woman of the house. The the folder of the Fall Wotan is in the laundry of the yard of the building in the next street, and the command is in need of it in the Old Town before the district of the city is cut in half, and there is not going to be another night."

"Janek. Your hand."

"The hand is nothing at all. The hand is the fourth time in the war." She saw him in the dark for a second: a boy of twenty-one with a rifle on his back and a bandage from the wrist to the elbow, and the other arm with a pistol in the hand of it, and the face of a young man who had been in the streets for six days. "There is a man of the command at the wall of the yard of the building on the far side of the next street, and he is going to be there for the hour from the three to the four, and the laundry of the woman with the horse is at this hour the only building in that yard with a floor in it, because the house of the yard burned on the fifth, and the woman with the horse is in the cellar of the furier's with her sister and the horse was taken by the men of the garrison on the second and there is nothing at all to be done about the horse." He put the paper through the gap of the door, and it was a folded page of a notebook, in a woman's hand, and the writing was in ink and there were three lines on it. "The the command says the paper is worth a company of men, and the command says the woman of the house is to have the thanks of the district of the rising of the city of Warsaw, and there is not going to be any document, and there is not going to be any name, and the command has said it in a room on the Barokowa at the ten o'clock, and there were four men in the room and a priest and a woman of the age of sixty in a soldier's coat who is the courier of the command and who was in the room and said nothing at all and cried in the third row with the rifle on her knees."

The boy went over the wall again into the dark, and she stood with her back to the door of the garden of the house of the General of the intelligence of the district of Warsaw in the middle of the night of the seventh of August 1944, with a page of a notebook in her hand.

She read it by the dark in the passage with the light of a match under the stair, and it said, in a woman's hand, three lines:

*The road to the kitchen of the building of the next street is by the wall of the yard, four metres on the eastern side, and the wall is broken at the height of the second brick course three metres from the corner, and the laundry of the woman with the horse is the third door on the left, and the boiler is at the end, and there is a an hour between the three and the four, and after the four the yard is under the fire of the machine gun of the garrison of the corner of the building on the Polna.*

*If the paper of the folder is in the hands of the command before the morning of the eighth, then the men of the armoured train and the men of the two trains of the last week of July are going to be at the Wilanów on the ninth of August and not on the ninth of the September, and the men of the line of the Vistula are going to be in the city on the ten, and the fourth of the objects of the folder is a paper that is worth the south of the city.*

*There is no obligation. The house of the woman of the Bagatela has done what there was to be done in the war. I have written this in my own hand, in a room on the Barokowa, at the ten o'clock in the evening of the seventh of August, and there is no name, and there is not going to be a name, and the two of us have never once been in the same room.*

There was no signature.

She read the three lines twice, standing in the dark under the stair of the house on the Bagatela with a burnt match in her fingers, and then she went up the stairs in the dark and lay down on the bed in the room on the first floor with her shoes on, because there was an hour between the three and the four, and it was twenty past three in the morning.

---

At half past three in the morning of the seventh of August, Krystyna Stankiewiczówna went down the back stairs of the house on the Bagatela in a pair of black shoes and a dark brown dress with no coat, and out through the door of the garden.

The garden was a rectangle of eleven metres by fourteen, with the walnut tree and a bench and a gravel path and a summer of dead leaves; and the wall of it was two metres of brick on the southern side, and the garden was in the dark, and the house behind her had three windows with a light in them and the rest in blackout, and on the first floor there was a man of the garrison sitting at the window of the passage with a rifle on the sill of it, and he was awake, and he had been awake for six days.

She got to the wall at the place where the brick course was broken, and put the toe of the shoe of the right foot into the gap, and the wall of the yard of the building in the next street was four metres on the other side, and the yard between the wall and the laundry was a space of eleven metres with a burned house on the northern side of it and a shed on the eastern, and there was no moon and there was firing at the corner of the Krucza, and there was firing in the direction of the Nowy Świat, and there was one German flare standing over the district of the Wola in the black sky, and the city smelled of burning.

She went over the wall.

She crossed the yard in eleven seconds, and she was not shot at, and there is no particular reason why she was not shot at, and it is the kind of thing that a person who has been in a war is never afterwards able to explain: the men of the machine gun on the roof of the house at the corner of the Polna were men of the garrison of the third company, and they had been shooting at every moving object in the yard for six nights, and in the eleven seconds of the seventh of August at half past three in the morning there was one moving object in the yard and they were asleep at the gun.

The laundry had no roof, which she had not known: the western end of it was gone, and the boiler was in the middle of a floor of broken brick with the rain of a an August week standing in the tubs, and the smell of the place was the smell of wet ash and soap, and it took her four minutes with her hands in the rubble to find the suitcase: a cheap cardboard case of the kind that the shops of the Marszałkowska sold for eleven zloty in 1943, in a space between the boiler and the wall, under a plank and eleven fur coats, and the coats were wet and them smelled of the burning of the fifth of August, and the suitcase was dry.

She opened it.

There were five sheets of the paper of the Fall Wotan of the district of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw, with the table of the rolling stock of the last week of July and the copy of the order of the 9th Army of the thirtieth of June 1944 and the map of the line of the Vistula; and there was a fold of brown paper, and in the fold there was the page of the eleven surnames of the Sicherheitsdienst of the district of Warsaw with four ticks against them, which she had put there on the twentieth of July and had not spoken of to any human being, because she had looked at the surnames for the four days of that week in the laundry office and had found, in the four days, that the surname in the middle of the eleven with the tick against it was *Zaleski*, and that the Zaleski of the register of the Sicherheitsdienst with the tick against his name was not the Piotr Zaleski of the kitchen of the house on the Mokotowska and the horse and the laundry and the cousin's wife; it was a Zaleski of the district of the Wola, of the age of fifty-one, of the profession of a chemist, and she had never once known what to do with it, and now she stood in the middle of a roofless laundry in the dark and took the page of the eleven surnames out of the fold of the brown paper and put it into her own hand.

She left it there, on the floor of the laundry, in the wet, in the dark: a page of paper of the card index of the Sicherheitsdienst of the district of Warsaw with eleven surnames on it and four ticks and one word in the hand of a general of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw, and the men of the German police of the district had been burning the index of that office in the courtyard of the aleja Szucha for four days, and there was not going to be one of them who would ever think of the floor of a laundry in the yard of the building in the next street; and the weight of the paper in the hand of the woman on the floor of the laundry was the weight of the two years.

She took the suitcase with the five sheets and went back across the yard.

She was two metres from the wall when the machine gun on the roof of the house at the corner of the Polna woke up and fired into the yard.

There is no describing it afterwards to anyone at all: the air of the yard full of a noise that is not a series of noises but a single object; the brick of the wall in front of her face coming away in pieces of the size of a fist; the top of the wall going out of it in a spray of lime and dust; and a woman of twenty-seven in a dark dress in the middle of the open ground with a suitcase in her right hand and the wall two metres in front of her face.

She put the suitcase on the ground and lay down on the gravel of the yard in the dust of the wall.

The gun stopped. Then it began again, and the burst went over the wall and into the garden of the house on the Bagatela, and the noise of it went into the windows of the house, and a man was shouting on the roof in German, and the gate of the garden opened.

She was up with the case in her hand and over the wall of the garden of the house of the General of the intelligence of the district of Warsaw in the four seconds, and the two metres of it was not possible and she was over it, and the suitcase went over it first and she came over it after, and the left knee of her was on the ground of a garden with a walnut tree in it and the gravel of the path in the face of the house, and there was a man in the dark in front of the door of the garden.

"Frau Generalin," said Phillip Brandt, in German, out loud, in the middle of the yard.

He had his pistol out of the holster and the barrel of it down his thigh, and he stood in the middle of the path of the garden in the light of the flare over the Wola, in a tunic with the sleeves rolled, and there were eleven men of the garrison at the windows of the house and a lieutenant of twenty-three from the town of Kassel coming out of the door of the kitchen.

"Eine Ratte," said Phillip Brandt, "mit einem Koffer. Ich habe geschossen."

*A rat, with a case. I fired.*

He put the pistol back in the holster and took the case out of her hand, and turned his body so that the men of the garrison were behind his shoulder, and stood with his back to the house and the case at his side, and said, in the ordinary voice of a captain of the ordnance of the district of Warsaw, to the boy-lieutenant of twenty-three from Kassel:

"Ich bringe das Ding zur Wache. Alle bleiben an den Fenstern."

*I'll take the thing to the guardroom. Everyone stays at the windows.*

And he walked with the case through the garden gate in the dark, in the ordinary way of a captain of the district carrying an object of no importance at half past three in the morning of the seventh day of a rising; and she stood in the middle of the garden of the house of the General of the intelligence of the district of Warsaw, with the dust of the wall in her hair and the taste of lime in her mouth, and looked at his back going through the gate, and did the one piece of arithmetic that she had not done in the four years, and the arithmetic was this:

*He fired at the yard. He fired at the yard before I was over the wall, and if the burst had been thirty centimetres to the left, I would be on the gravel of the yard now with the case beside my hand, and no man in the history of the German army is ever going to be able to explain what a captain of the ordnance of the district of Warsaw was doing in the garden of the house of the General of the intelligence service of the district at half past three in the morning with his pistol out of the holster.*

He came back at four.

He came back through the gate of the garden with his hands empty and his cap in his belt, and the lodge at the corner of the Polna, and the men of the guard of the district of the intelligence service of the district of Warsaw were sitting in the passage of the house with their rifles between their knees, and the boy-lieutenant of twenty-three from Kassel was asleep in the chair of the drawing room of the Countess Jadwiga Ostrowska with his mouth open, and there was nobody at all in the house who was awake.

"He has it?" she said, in Polish, in the passage.

"He has it."

"And the boy?"

"There is a man of the command at the wall of the yard of the building in the next street," said Phillip Brandt, in the dark, "and there was a boy of twenty-one with a bandage on his right arm at the corner of the Novum at the four, and the case was in the hand of the boy at the four and eleven minutes, and the boy was in the mouth of the passage of the Przejazd when the machine gun of the building of the insurance company on the Polna opened on the mouth of the passage at the four and the quarter, and I am a captain of the German army and I was in the street with the men of the third company of the garrison at the four and the quarter, and there is a boy of twenty-one with a bandage on his right arm in the middle of the street in front of the mouth of the passage, and the case was under him, and he was not able to be got at, because there was a machine gun on the roof and the men of the third company were shooting at the mouth of the passage for the eleven minutes from the four and the quarter to the four and the twenty-six minutes, and the boy was on the pavement of the Przejazd in the middle of the street of the city of Warsaw with a case under his body and a bandage on his right arm, and there is not one thing in the four years that I have been able to do, and I am the man who was standing in the mouth of the passage of the house of the General of the district with a pistol in my hand, and I did nothing at all, in the eleven minutes, and the case is in the middle of the street of the Przejazd, and it is going to be in the middle of the street of the Przejazd in the morning, and there is nobody at all in the world who is going to be able to pick it up."

She stood in the dark of the passage of the house of the General of the intelligence of the district of Warsaw at a quarter to five in the morning of the seventh of August 1944, and she did not cry, because there was a garrison in the house, and because a woman who is going to be in a chair by a window at eleven o'clock at night does not cry in a passage with a company of infantry in the next room.

There are four hundred thousand of us in this district and eleven thousand in the Pawiak and two hundred thousand in the Wola and one boy of twenty-one in the middle of the street of the Przejazd with a suitcase under his body, and there is a page of the card index of the Sicherheitsdienst of the district of Warsaw on the floor of the laundry of the yard of the building in the next street, and there is a woman of the household of the General of the intelligence of the district of Warsaw standing in the passage of the house on the Bagatela at a quarter to five in the morning with the blood of the city on the floor of the world, and the arithmetic of it is:

*There is nothing to be done. There is nothing at all to be done. There is not one item in the column. The the column is empty. The the column has been empty since the corridor of a house on the Konopnickiej in the November of 1942, and the reason that I have been able to do it for four years is that I have never once permitted myself to look at the column.*
